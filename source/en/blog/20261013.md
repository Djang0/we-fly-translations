## Goodbye Alpha, Hello Beta

Since our first alpha, We-Fly has changed shape almost every week. New pages appeared, old ones moved, and the [first shiny release](/doc/whats-new?post=20261003) even swapped the whole navigation. That was the deal with an alpha: everything could still move.

With **version 1.0.0-beta.1**, that phase is over. Beta means the foundations are in place for 1.0:

- **No more big reshuffles.** The pages, the navigation and your logbook keep their shape. Your bookmarks and shared links keep working.
- **Polish over novelty.** The coming releases focus on fixing rough edges, speeding things up and finishing what's there, rather than adding a new feature every week.
- **Your feedback weighs more than ever.** Anything that feels odd, slow or wrong now is exactly what we want to hear before 1.0.

And to open the beta, we picked the feature many of you asked for at landing: **We-Fly in your own language.**

## Six Languages, One Logbook

We-Fly now speaks **English, French, German, Dutch, Italian and Spanish**.

Picking yours takes a second:

- Signed in, open your **avatar menu** and choose **Language**. You'll also find a **Language** row in your profile's display settings.
- On the public pages (home, takeoffs, docs, blog), tap the **globe** in the top bar.

We-Fly remembers your choice on your account, so your phone, your laptop and the tablet in your harness bag all follow along.

## What Got Translated

Pretty much everything a pilot touches:

- **Your logbook and every flight.** Filters, bulk actions, the whole flight page (summary, phases, thermals, wind, speed, vario, G-force, weather, competition task, notes), the 2D and 3D replay and the same-day comparison.
- **The Cockpit, Success and your season recap.**
- **The XC planner**, all four steps: route and task design with its checks and tips, airspaces with the vertical cut, NOTAMs and the briefing.
- **Community and takeoffs.** Feeds, pilots, leaderboards, clubs, challenges, **Place to be**, the flight search map, every takeoff page with its flyability verdict, and the public pages you share.
- **Sign in, Profile and Upload**, error messages included. When a file is refused, you now learn why in your own words.
- **All 28 help articles**, with buttons and menus named exactly as you see them on screen. Search engines list each article in every language, so a German pilot searching for help finds the German page.
- **Emails.** Account and password emails, review requests, tips to get started and data retention notices arrive in your language. So do the messages your **safety contacts** get when you take off, land or raise an alert: the person waiting for you at home reads them in your language too.
- **Release notes.** The **What's New** window and the history on the [About](/about) page are translated from version 1.0.0-alpha.1 on.

The privacy policy, terms of service and data retention policy are translated as well. The English text remains the one that legally applies, and those pages say so.

## Numbers the Way You Write Them

Translating words was only half the job. A French pilot writes **7,8 km** and **1 850 m**, a German one **1.850 m**. We-Fly now follows your language for decimal commas, thousands separators, dates, weekdays and month names, everywhere from the logbook to the charts, while still respecting your unit settings. Want feet and knots in Spanish? No problem.

Clock times stay on a 24-hour clock, because nobody wants to work out whether their launch was at 1 pm or 1 am.

## Written by Pilots, for Pilots

A translation that sounds like a washing machine manual is no help on launch. So we set a few rules:

- **We talk like pilots.** It's *tu* in French and Italian, *du* in German, *je* in Dutch and *tú* in Spanish, the way the free flight community talks.
- **The jargon is real jargon.** A shared glossary pins down how each language says thermal, glide ratio, speed bar, top landing or low save, so the same word means the same thing on every page.
- **Pages stay light.** Each page only loads the text it actually shows. The Docs pages carry about 60% less text than before, your profile about 85% less, which helps on a weak signal at the foot of the hill.

## Help Us Translate We-Fly

Here's the honest part: the first drafts were prepared with the help of AI, then checked against our glossary and style guides. They're good, but they're not yet what a local pilot would say over a beer after a big day. That's where you come in, and you don't need to be a developer or a professional translator. If you fly and you speak one of these languages, you're exactly who we need.

### The easy way: just tell us

Spotted a word that sounds off? Send us a message via [Contact](/contact) with three things: **the page**, **what it says** and **what it should say**. A screenshot helps. We'll make the change for you.

### The hands-on way: edit the translations yourself

Every translation lives in a public repository: [we-fly-translations on GitHub](https://github.com/Djang0/we-fly-translations). A free GitHub account is all you need, everything happens in your browser.

1. **Read the ground rules first.** The [glossary](https://github.com/Djang0/we-fly-translations/blob/main/glossary.yaml) lists how each language says thermal, glide ratio, speed bar and friends, and your language's [style guide](https://github.com/Djang0/we-fly-translations/tree/main/style) sets the tone (*tu*, *du*, *je*, *tú*).
2. **Find the text.** Each language has its own folders:
   - `messages/<language>/` holds the app's buttons, labels and messages, one file per area (`flights.json`, `cockpit.json`, `xcPlanner.json`...).
   - `docs/<language>/` holds the help articles.
   - `blog/<language>/` holds blog posts, this one included.
   - `changelog/<language>.json` holds the release notes.

   Not sure which file? Use GitHub's search box with the exact words you see on screen.
3. **Edit and propose.** Open the file, click the **pencil** icon, change the wording and press **Propose changes**. GitHub turns that into a pull request for us to review.
4. **Keep the placeholders.** Anything in curly braces such as `{count}` or `{name}`, and tags such as `<b>...</b>`, must stay exactly as they are; only translate the words around them. Numbers and units never go in the text, they come from each pilot's unit settings. An automatic check flags a broken placeholder before anything goes live.

Think a glossary term is wrong for your community? Propose the change to `glossary.yaml` on its own, with a short explanation, so every page can follow. The full checklist is in the repository's [contributing guide](https://github.com/Djang0/we-fly-translations/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md).

### What happens next

We review your proposal, merge it, and it goes live with the **next We-Fly release**. Translations are shared under the [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) licence and credited to the We-Fly translators, so your work stays open for the whole free flight community.

Not on GitHub and don't want to be? A friendlier, click-to-suggest editor is on its way. Until then, the [Contact](/contact) route works just as well.

A few things deliberately stay in English for now: blog posts from before today, release notes older than 1.0.0-alpha.1, and the developer API documentation.
